## 1. Math

I attended a math program called MathIly-Er for 5 weeks this summer, and it was one of the best times of my life. We learned so much math, here’s the daily breakdown:

Breakfast then 4 hour morning class (9:00 am – 1:00 pm), Lunch, Daily Gather (a presentation on a different type of math each day, usually by a college professor, from 4:00-5:30), Dinner, then Problem Session (6:00-9:00). So a total of over 8 hours a day of structured math, 6 days a week, for 5 weeks.

The biggest thing that I obtained from this math program is the patience and passion to learn. When you sit down for 8 hours a day thinking about how math could work, studying for 2-3 hours seems like a fine thing to do.

I also learned to “keep my brain on”. One of the ideas stressed by our instructors is that it is crucial keep our brains on when we leave camp. To keep on wondering, to trying, to conjecturing, to proving, and seeing math happen–it’s so important.

From an introspection near the beginning of camp: _“My goal is to come to see and observe how the math world operates, and to see how I like it. I haven’t ever spent entire days on math, so getting used to thinking deeply for so long has taken a while. But I think that I have been enjoying being here a lot. Every day I get intrigued by something, and for the first time in my life, I’ve been losing sleep over thinking about math. I literally lay in bed for the night and my head randomly thinks of some sequence or idea that we talked about throughout the day. Even outside of class, I’ve been influenced by my friends and roommates to learn how to code, play sudoku and set, and even play math games such as krypto outside of class._“

Math had been active in my head so much that it was near impossible to turn off and fall sleep. And checking in with the other campers, this experience of closing your eyes and being back in morning lecture was not just a me thing. It was practically everyone.

Now let’s talk about the people. Math people like to study beautiful ideas in life. I wrote in my final introspection: “_I came here to learn more about math people and the math world, but I’ve concluded that there’s too much math and too many different types of mathematicians for me to even make this goal. For example, one of my roommates was losing sleep over a new competition math problem (IMO) because it was “just too fun”. One of my other roommates was bored one day and read a linear algebra book. Every mathematician has their own preferences and styles of math that are directly influenced by their everyday life_.” Continuing on, “_I was extremely lucky and happy with my roommates, and built a close bond with them; we shared pretty much every idea and its execution together. I think that they made the time fly by here for me._“

Since MathILy-Er, I’ve been more interested in math than ever before. A true, honest desire to want to learn more, see more, and do more math. I want to be involved in my math community, I want to go to Wednesday Math Tea, I want to talk about math with other people.

Here’s the list I supplied at the end of my last introspection for what I did outside of class at MathILy-Er:

- I took over 30 naps and slept under my bed frame
- My roommates got me to start learning how to code, and I coded a website after learning HTML
- That one day that the path to dinner was blocked by a tree and we got pizza for dinner, Alan and I learned to juggle and our pod got our only 25 (perfect game) in hanabi
- We did a ton of wall sits and while playing krypto
- We played some video games while commenting on how much of a waste of time it was
- I ran around 240 miles through 800 0.3 mile laps around the soccer and lacrosse field
- We skipped rocks in the river after the river flooded
- We learned that bouncing a lacrosse ball inside our dorm room amplified through the floor and was more loud for our neighbors than ourselves
- We ate 12 packs of popcorn during branch
- We laughed a ton and never got into any non-mathematical arguments

## 2. Coding

If there was any cheat skill in life, it would be coding. I’m interested in coding because it forces us to dissect what we see and encounter in everyday life into small, manageable chunks to solve problems and find solutions. Computers nowadays are able to go through thousands of inputs each second to see what works, what fails, or what worked the best. We modeled many problems at MathILy-Er into matrices and used sage (python) to plug and chug through all the combinations to see if something was possible. But coding is not a skill that I’m adept at, and there’s that iceberg effect, as coding only gets more and more complex each day we live.

I have learned python through an online free course by the University of Helsinki, which I found to be incredible. I do want to learn c++, HTML, and javascript when I get free time. But free time is quite valuable these days.

I’m also interested in github and how coding at higher levels go. Coding is a mostly problem solving task, and no one is adept at everything, so coding is almost always a group project, which I enjoy.

There’s a certain satisfaction in coding something that works properly and being able to understand how code will behave. The best thing that I’ve coded so far is a tic-tac-toe game, or perhaps a daily planner (both in python).

Coding is for sure something that I will want to understand for the future. I plan to perhaps minor in computer science or self study programming as I get older, or find more time.

## 3. Physics

Sticking with the math-ier topics, I’ve recently been interested in Physics. I have never taken physics before nor have I learned anything about it, but I do feel that it is my duty as a human to strive to understand this world. After all, if we don’t make every attempt to understand this world we live in, wouldn’t all we be doing is responding to unknown light and stimuli?

If I were to want to simulate a world, I would want to create laws and constants that this world would abide by. With my limited understanding of what physics is, I feel that physics are these laws that, if our life is simulated, our creator(s) did a splendid job creating.

And I want to learn more about the world. Physics deals a whole lot with the world. So I want to learn more about Physics.

I plan to read the Giancoli book and spend my 2nd period open with my school’s physics teacher, Mr. Miller.

Physics is another one of these topics where talking about it with other people is so important and worthwhile. I mean, you’re literally discussing what the rules of the world are.

## 7. Philosophy and Thinking

I’m known best for taking advanced math courses. But what I believe I’m most gifted in, is thinking abstractly and thinking about life. I’ve thought of so many ideas and want to talk about them with other people. Questions like:

- What’s the best way to teach a child intuition?
- How is it possible to go from zero to one, nothing to something?
- When do you quit something you love?

Here’s one of my favorite questions: what am I really? Well this is a question that I thought of when I was in bed many years ago. I gave the generic, I’m a human, a boy, named Ben, black hair, black eyes… But then I ran out of words that categorized who I am, but the question was still “What am I?”. So when the honest answer of “I don’t know what I am” comes out and “I don’t know what’ll happen to me” and “I don’t know where I am right now, or where my ideas are coming from and will go”, a void opens up in your chest and it gives me a feeling of missing people. When I first felt this void, I got out of bed and went downstairs to just sit with my mom, as all I wanted to do was feel like something.

Anyway, I enjoy thinking about tough questions and feeling these strange yet vivid emotions. I enjoy dealing with ideas such as “endless”, “best”, and “future”, yet also love the present and the endless number of moments captured (think of a camera shutter) between the seconds. And again, thinking requires time which I’m really short on now.

## 8. Building Relationships

This point ties all of these passions together. I feel that it is essential to talk about what you are passionate about with people that are passionate about the same thing. These people that you end up learning with are probably some of the most important people you’ll ever meet. So I want to invest myself and time into relationships like this.
